# Existing Catalogue → Modern-History Channel Transition

**Purpose:** retain useful legacy videos while making the channel's future promise unmistakable: evidence-led history from the 1800s onward.

**Evidence limitation:** the only catalogue lead available during this planning pass is a third-party indexed mirror that links to the supplied YouTube channel ID. It is stale and is not an authoritative current upload list. Therefore this document defines a repeatable classification method and channel structure; it does not claim to have reviewed every live upload.

## 1. Do not mass-delete or mass-rebrand old videos

Keep existing uploads unless there is a specific factual, rights, privacy or policy reason to unlist, edit or remove one. Older videos can continue to attract search traffic. Do not change titles, thumbnails, descriptions, privacy or playlists in bulk without reviewing each video and obtaining the channel owner's approval.

## 2. Classify each existing upload

Use the following catalogue labels in a working spreadsheet:

- **CORE — 1800s onward:** directly supports the new editorial promise.
- **BRIDGE — earlier history with modern relevance:** useful context for a later modern-history episode, but not the main channel promise.
- **ARCHIVE — pre-1800s:** retain, but place in a clearly labelled legacy playlist.
- **REVIEW — uncertain:** title, date, subject or historical claims need checking.
- **HOLD — rights or policy concern:** do not promote until reviewed.

For every video, record: exact title, URL, publication date, historical period, geography, topic, current views, 28-day views if available, watch time, average view duration, traffic source, playlist, end screen destination, rights status, factual review status and proposed action.

Do not infer performance from a third-party mirror's view count. Use YouTube Studio for private metrics.

## 3. Recommended channel homepage

Arrange sections in this order:

1. **Start Here — Modern History Explained** (channel trailer or strongest current 1800s-onward episode)
2. **Latest Documentaries**
3. **Wars That Changed the World** (playlist)
4. **Intelligence, Espionage & Political Decisions** (playlist)
5. **Forgotten Fronts & Under-covered Conflicts** (playlist)
6. **Africa: Conflict, Empire & Independence** (playlist)
7. **Revolutions, States & Power** (playlist)
8. **Military Strategy, Logistics & Technology** (playlist)
9. **The Archive: Earlier World History** (legacy playlist, clearly labelled)

Only create a playlist section once it has enough relevant videos to be useful. Avoid empty playlists and do not misclassify a video just to populate a section.

## 4. Playlist rules

- One primary playlist per video; add a secondary playlist only when it genuinely serves a different viewing journey.
- Playlist titles should describe the subject, not promise unsupported outcomes.
- Playlist descriptions should explain the period, geographic scope and editorial angle.
- Order episodes to create a logical journey: context → causes → decisions → campaign/events → consequences.
- Add new episodes to the relevant playlist immediately after publication.
- Keep pre-1800s uploads in the archive rather than mixing them into the main modern-history series.

## 5. About-section draft

> Untapped History explores the decisions, conflicts and turning points that shaped the modern world—from the nineteenth century onward.
>
> Through carefully researched documentaries, we examine military campaigns, political crises, intelligence, strategy, logistics, revolutions and the human consequences of power.
>
> Our coverage is global. We look beyond familiar battlefields to include overlooked conflicts, underrepresented perspectives and the evidence behind contested historical stories.
>
> Every episode aims to separate documented facts from disputed claims and popular myths.

Review the channel's exact public name and contact details in YouTube Studio before publishing this text. Do not invent a business email.

## 6. Banner wording

Suggested concise banner line:

**MODERN HISTORY. GLOBAL PERSPECTIVES. EVIDENCE OVER MYTH.**

This is a positioning statement, not a claim of institutional affiliation or academic endorsement.

## 7. Review sequence for each legacy upload

1. Confirm the video is actually published on the target channel.
2. Record its exact title and URL.
3. Identify the historical period and geography.
4. Check whether its central claims remain defensible.
5. Check whether its visuals, music and narration have documented usage rights.
6. Review YouTube Studio performance at comparable video ages where possible.
7. Assign CORE, BRIDGE, ARCHIVE, REVIEW or HOLD.
8. Decide whether to keep as-is, add to a playlist, improve metadata, or hold for deeper review.
9. Make changes only after the owner approves the specific edits.
10. For every CORE video, add a relevant next-watch destination where available.

## 8. Suggested spreadsheet columns

`Video title | URL | Published date | Period | Region | Topic | Classification | Views | 28-day views | Watch time | AVD | Main traffic source | Primary playlist | End screen destination | Rights status | Fact-check status | Proposed action | Approval status`

## 9. Immediate priorities

- Create the legacy archive playlist for pre-1800s material.
- Build the homepage around the modern-history promise.
- Link each new documentary to one relevant older CORE upload or playlist; do not force unrelated recommendations.
- Review the existing catalogue before changing metadata.
