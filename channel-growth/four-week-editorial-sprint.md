# Untapped History & Storytelling — Four-Week Editorial Sprint

**Planning basis:** 4 weeks; 2 long-form documentaries and 1 Short each week.  
**Editorial boundary:** political, military and world history from the 1800s onward.  
**Research standard:** evidence-led, globally balanced, neutral in tone. Treat disputed claims as disputed; distinguish contemporary records, later scholarship and popular retellings.

This is a publishing plan, not a performance forecast. Topics and titles are proposals; dates should be assigned only after production capacity and source checks are confirmed.

## Channel promise

Untapped History explains how decisions, logistics, intelligence, geography, technology and human consequences shaped modern history. Each episode should answer a specific historical question—not merely retell a battle chronologically.

Keep older ancient/medieval uploads in the catalogue if they already exist. Organise them into a clearly labelled archive playlist; make the channel banner, About text, playlists and future uploads consistently communicate the 1800s-onward focus.

### Release order and production rule

- Publish Long-form A first, then Long-form B, with the Short between or after them where production allows.
- Do not publish a topic simply to meet the calendar if its core claims, maps, footage rights or narration are not cleared.
- Winter War is already in production. Keep its release on HOLD until human listening review, final video/caption synchronization, historical-map approval and scene-asset rights review are complete.
- If a later episode is not ready, move the slate forward. Do not fill a gap with generic AI visuals, repetitive still-image pans or unsourced claims.

## Packaging rules

For every long-form upload, prepare three materially different title approaches before choosing one:

1. **Question / mystery:** a precise historical question the episode answers.
2. **Decision / consequence:** the decision that changed the course of events.
3. **Mechanism / explanation:** the logistics, intelligence, geography or technology that explains the outcome.

Thumbnail rules:
- One central subject or visual conflict; no cluttered collage.
- Two to four words maximum, readable at mobile size.
- Do not repeat the full title on the thumbnail.
- Use dates only when they clarify the historical setting.
- No fabricated quotations, casualty figures, maps, uniforms or weapons.
- Thumbnail imagery must be rights-cleared or original and must not imply a scene that did not happen.

Description rules:
- First two lines state the historical question and the episode’s answer/value.
- Add a concise summary, chapters only after final edit timing, primary/secondary source list, image/footage credits and relevant playlist link.
- Separate confirmed facts from disputed interpretations.
- Do not stuff tags or repeat keywords unnaturally.

## Research and production gate

Every episode must pass these gates before publication:

1. **Question:** one clear question and a defensible answer.
2. **Source base:** at least two independent reputable sources for major interpretive claims; use primary sources where they add evidence, not as decoration.
3. **Claim register:** dates, numbers, quotations, attributions and causal claims recorded with source and confidence.
4. **Global perspective:** include the perspectives of the states and populations directly involved; do not default to a Western-only account.
5. **Narrative:** establish stakes early, explain causes before consequences, and introduce a meaningful new piece of evidence or explanation regularly.
6. **Visual accuracy:** maps, borders, uniforms, equipment and locations checked against the period.
7. **Rights:** retain provider, model, licence, source URL and commercial-use evidence for every external visual/audio asset. Unclear rights means HOLD.
8. **Audio and captions:** listen to the complete narration; correct pronunciation and artefacts; synchronize captions to the final picture lock.
9. **Packaging:** title and thumbnail accurately represent the video and make one clear promise.
10. **End screen and cards:** add only relevant destinations after the next video or playlist exists; verify clickable elements in YouTube Studio after upload.

## Monthly measurement sheet

Record these from YouTube Studio for each long-form video at 7-day and 28-day age. Keep Shorts separate.

| Metric | What it tells us | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions and impressions click-through rate | Whether the topic and packaging earn clicks | Test title/thumbnail only when there is enough impression volume to interpret |
| Views and unique viewers | Reach | Compare videos at the same age, not lifetime totals |
| Average view duration and average percentage viewed | Viewing depth | Inspect the retention graph for specific drop-offs |
| First 30-second retention | Whether opening promise matches delivery | Rewrite future openings if viewers leave before the central question is established |
| Watch time (hours) | Long-form viewing contribution | Track separately from Shorts-feed watch time |
| Traffic sources and YouTube search terms | Discovery route and audience intent | Build follow-up topics from demonstrated demand |
| End-screen element click rate | Whether viewers continue to another video | Improve destination relevance and the final verbal/visual handoff |
| Returning viewers and subscribers gained | Audience development | Look for repeat viewing across related episodes |

Do not compare a Short’s views or retention directly with a long-form documentary. Do not use third-party public view counts as a substitute for private Studio metrics.

## Four-week experiment design

Run one principal packaging test at a time; record the change date and compare at equivalent video ages.

- **Test 1 — Specific promise:** compare a broad event title with a title naming the episode’s central question or mechanism.
- **Test 2 — Opening contract:** state the central question, stakes and evidence basis in the first 20–30 seconds; avoid generic cinematic introductions.
- **Test 3 — Evidence-led visual rhythm:** use maps, documents, verified archival imagery and original explanatory graphics when they add information. Every visual beat must advance the explanation.
- **Test 4 — Next-video journey:** connect related episodes through playlists, end screens and a spoken handoff. Do not send viewers to an unrelated upload.

Change one major packaging variable at a time where possible. Keep a record of the hypothesis, baseline, change, observation window and result. A small sample is inconclusive—not proof of success or failure.
