**Promise:** The wars, power struggles and decisions that shaped our world.

## 1. Goal math — calculate before forecasting

The goals are:
- 8,000 eligible public long-form watch hours within 120–240 days.
- At least 4,500 views per long-form upload.
- At least 5:00 average view duration per long-form video.
- 250,000 total channel views per month.

At exactly 4,500 eligible long-form views and exactly 5:00 average view duration:
- Per long video: 22,500 watched minutes = **375 watch hours**.
- Two long videos/week: **750 watch hours/week**, if both videos reach 4,500 eligible views and retain a 5-minute average duration.
- 120 days = 17.14 weeks; approximately 34 long videos; modelled total = **12,750 watch hours** (34 × 375), or 12,857 hours using fractional weekly cadence.
- 240 days = 34.29 weeks; approximately 69 long videos; modelled total = **25,875 watch hours** (69 × 375), or 25,714 hours using fractional weekly cadence.
- 8,000 hours at a 5-minute average duration requires **96,000 eligible long-form views**.
- That equals 21.34 videos at 4,500 eligible views each, assuming all watch time is eligible and the full 5-minute average is actually achieved.

These are arithmetic scenarios, not predictions. They assume each upload reaches 4,500 views within the measurement window, every counted view contributes an average of five minutes, the channel has no unlisted/private/deleted exclusions, and the videos are public long-form. Uploads published late in the window have less time to accumulate views. Back-catalogue views can add watch time. Actual Studio analytics must replace the assumptions as soon as the correct channel is connected.

### 250,000 monthly views is a separate, much harder scale target

At two long videos per week, a 30-day month contains about 8.57 new long-form uploads. If each reaches 4,500 views during that month, new long-form uploads contribute approximately **38,565 views**. The remaining **211,435 monthly views** must come from the back catalogue, Shorts and other eligible public formats. Shorts can help total views and discovery, but Shorts-feed watch time does not count toward the long-form public-watch-hours threshold. Do not combine the two metrics.

A useful monthly dashboard therefore tracks separately:
1. Eligible public long-form watch hours.
2. Long-form views and engaged views.
3. Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
4. Shorts engaged views and viewed-vs-swiped-away where available.
5. Total monthly channel views.
6. Returning viewers, subscribers gained, impressions, CTR by traffic source and upload age.
7. Search, Browse, Suggested, External and playlist traffic.

## 2. Editorial differentiation — compete on insight, not imitation

Public competitor research reviewed on 5 November 2026 is a directional format study, not private analytics or a complete content audit.

### Reference channels and observable strengths

| Channel | Publicly observable format strength | What to learn | Differentiation opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Epic History TV | Dramatic, fact-led, relatively concise history films; creator describes researching, writing and animating the videos personally | Clear narrative arc, focused topic, strong maps/animation, disciplined storytelling | Give every episode a specific contested decision, logistics problem, overlooked witness or consequence that standard campaign summaries often compress |
| The Operations Room | Animated, time-sequenced battle operations; creator describes time-lapse operational animation and a deliberate production cadence | Spatial clarity, chronology, operational cause-and-effect | Combine tactical clarity with political context, civilian consequences, strategic uncertainty and explicit source confidence |
| Kings and Generals | Detailed maps, graphics and animation; broad military-history coverage and long-form series | Map literacy, campaign sequencing, series continuity | Cover underrepresented regions, smaller states, coalition politics, supply and command failure without defaulting to battle-by-battle recitation |
| Timeline — World History Documentaries | Large documentary catalogue and broad historical subject mix | Topic breadth, packaging of recognizable historical subjects, catalogue depth | Build a coherent channel identity around decisions, power and consequences from 1800 onward; use fewer generic “full history” surveys |
| Real Time History | Focused campaign and conflict documentaries with a specialist historical lens | Narrowly scoped episodes and campaign context | Make the “why this outcome?” question explicit, test popular explanations against evidence, and distinguish what is known from disputed |

Public research supports the formats above; it does not prove a competitor weakness. Treat “gaps” below as hypotheses to test against comments, topic coverage and audience response, not claims that competitors never do the work.

### Channel's own editorial advantage

Build a repeatable but materially varied identity:
- **Decision under pressure:** what leaders knew, what they misunderstood, what options existed and what each choice cost.
- **The system behind the battle:** logistics, geography, industry, intelligence, weather, communications, political legitimacy and coalition constraints.
- **The overlooked front:** Africa, Asia, Eastern Europe, the Middle East, Latin America and smaller states, not only familiar Western-European and US narratives.
- **The evidence test:** compare popular claims with primary records and reputable scholarship; identify disputed or unknowable details.
- **The human consequence:** civilian displacement, occupation, prisoners, disease, shortages and postwar political effects, treated respectfully and without shock exploitation.

Do not copy competitors' titles, thumbnails, scripts, maps, animations, music, editing signatures or footage. Learn the underlying audience mechanism and make original expression.

## 3. Cinematic storytelling standard

Every long documentary must be built around one central question and a defensible answer.

### Narrative architecture
1. **0:00–0:20 — Cold open:** begin inside a consequential moment, dilemma or contradiction. No channel greeting, logo sequence or generic preamble.
2. **0:20–0:45 — Promise and stakes:** tell viewers what mystery or decision the film will resolve and why it matters.
3. **0:45–1:30 — Context only:** establish place, date, actors and the constraints needed to understand the story.
4. **Main body — escalating causal chain:** each chapter changes the viewer's understanding. Alternate human-scale scenes, maps, operational explanation, primary-source evidence and consequences.
5. **Every 45–75 seconds — new information or payoff:** introduce a verified fact, reversal, decision, constraint, consequence or visual explanation. Do not force a beat if the evidence does not support it.
6. **Final 60–90 seconds — resolution:** answer the opening question, identify what remains disputed and explain the longer consequence.
7. **Final 5–20 seconds — end screen:** preserve uncluttered visual space and a spoken, topic-relevant next-viewing invitation.

### Visual rules
- Use genuine moving footage or original animation that explains something. No photo slideshow, static image zoom, generic stock filler or repetitive AI clip sequence.
- Every shot must have a reason to exist and match the narration's time, place and claim.
- Use maps when geography or movement changes the explanation; show date, scale limitations, source and disputed borders where applicable.
- Re-enactments must not be presented as archival footage. No fabricated readable documents, fake quotes, invented insignia or false historical likenesses.
- Sound design supports comprehension; silence is preferable to unlicensed music or effects.
- Maintain a shot-level rights and accuracy register. Any uncertain asset remains HOLD.

## 4. Packaging and thumbnail standard

A model score of 95/100 is not a 95% click-through rate and cannot guarantee impressions or views. Use scoring tools only as directional creative feedback. Actual CTR is measured in YouTube Studio and must be segmented by traffic source, impressions volume and upload age.

Thumbnail requirements:
- One dominant subject and one legible visual idea, readable on a small phone screen.
- Clear subject separation, strong light/dark contrast, restrained palette and no clutter.
- Title and thumbnail complement each other rather than repeat the same words.
- Make a specific truthful promise; do not use fabricated scenes as proof of a real event.
- Use a historically plausible focal subject and verify uniforms, equipment, flags, geography and dates.
- Prepare two genuinely different concepts where practical; compare with YouTube's native test feature if available, and choose from actual watch-time share/performance rather than subjective certainty.
- Never guarantee a score, CTR or view count.

## 5. SEO and discovery workflow

### Before scripting
- Start with a topic hypothesis, audience question and primary search phrase.
- Research search demand, competition and adjacent questions; record geography, language and date of the data.
- Review current top search results and relevant videos: title promise, format, upload age, channel scale, view velocity where available and comments that reveal unresolved questions.
- Choose topics with an intersection of audience interest, historical evidence, visual explainability and channel differentiation.
- Do not select a topic on search volume alone.

### Before publishing
- Title: primary entity/topic plus a clear consequence, question or tension; natural language, accurate, not keyword-stuffed.
- Description: first two lines clearly state the subject and viewer payoff; then a concise factual summary, chapter list, source notes, relevant playlist and next-video links.
- Tags: a small set of accurate variants and entities, within YouTube's character limit; tags are not a substitute for a strong topic/title/thumbnail.
- Chapters: accurate, useful and synchronized to final edit.
- Playlist: place each upload into a tightly related series or topic playlist.
- Pinned comment: invite a substantive historical question and link the next relevant episode where appropriate.
- Cards: use only relevant videos/playlists at the point the narration references the related subject; avoid interrupting a crucial sentence or visual.
- End screen: design the final 5–20 seconds into the edit, normally one relevant next video or playlist plus Subscribe. Check that it is actually saved in Studio after upload.
- Short: derive one standalone question/payoff from the long documentary; add a natural related-video link where YouTube supports it. Never treat Shorts-feed watch time as long-form eligibility.

### After publishing
- Review at 24–48 hours, 7 days and 28 days. Compare at similar upload ages, not just calendar totals.
- Low impressions: topic/audience demand or channel distribution hypothesis; do not immediately rewrite title.
- Impressions but weak CTR: test packaging; segment by Browse, Suggested and Search and avoid overreacting to small samples.
- Strong CTR but weak first-minute retention: title/thumbnail promise mismatch or opening failure.
- Strong retention but low reach: topic demand, audience matching, competition and distribution need investigation.
- Search impressions but low search clicks: improve exact topic clarity and packaging; do not stuff keywords.
- Review card click rate and end-screen element click rate; improve the next-video journey.
- Maintain an experiment log: hypothesis, change, date, sample size, result and next action.

## 6. Weekly publishing rhythm

A sustainable starting schedule in Africa/Johannesburg:
- **Monday:** Long documentary 1.
- **Wednesday:** One Short derived from an original insight in the upcoming or recent documentary.
- **Friday:** Long documentary 2.

This is a planning cadence, not a claim that these are proven optimal audience hours. Use channel-specific viewer activity and actual upload results to adjust the times. If quality, research or rights gates are not met, delay the upload rather than publish a weak or risky episode.

## 7. End screens and cards — compulsory production checklist

For every long-form upload:
- Reserve the final 20 seconds in the edit for the end screen; avoid important subtitles, key map labels or essential action underneath overlays.
- Add a relevant “best for viewer” or specific next episode/playlist element and a Subscribe element. Use a specific video when it creates a strong narrative continuation.
- Add cards at natural contextual moments before the final end-screen period; link only to closely relevant videos/playlists.
- After saving in YouTube Studio, verify the elements in the editor and record a screenshot or checklist confirmation in the release record.
- At 7 and 28 days, record card impressions/clicks/click rate and end-screen impressions/clicks/click rate.
- If no relevant next video exists yet, use a topic playlist rather than a random unrelated video.

A planned end screen is not a configured end screen. Never mark the release checklist complete until verified in Studio.

## 8. Policy, copyright and disclosure gate

- No guarantee of zero policy risk is possible. Use a conservative pre-publication review and HOLD uncertain material.
- Original research, writing, narration, substantive analysis and meaningful editorial value must be clear. Avoid mass-produced/repetitive or interchangeable AI content.
- Verify commercial rights for every clip, still, map, sound, font and other asset. Historical age does not automatically make a recording public domain.
- Respect copyright, privacy, defamation and applicable laws. Keep licence evidence and source citations.
- Realistic AI-generated scenes depicting events/places that did not occur, or meaningful alterations of real events/places, require the appropriate YouTube altered/synthetic-content disclosure. Do not hide the disclosure to protect reach; YouTube says the label alone does not reduce monetization eligibility or recommendations.
- Paid-promotion declaration: do not falsely mark a video as containing paid promotion when there is no paid placement, sponsorship or endorsement. But if a paid promotion exists and disclosure is required, declare it. The user's preference cannot override a legal or platform disclosure requirement.
- Never buy views, subscribers, watch hours or engagement, or use ad campaigns to inflate YPP eligibility. Views from ads do not count as qualified watch hours.
- Review monetization policy at channel level as well as video level, including originality, reused content, inauthentic/repetitive content, metadata and About page.

## 9. Monthly SEO and growth review

Run on the first Monday of each month. Use the latest complete 30-day period and the immediately preceding 30-day period, with exact dates and coverage stated.

Report:
- Channel identity and authorized-account match.
- Long-form and Shorts metrics separately.
- Eligible public long-form watch hours toward the 8,000-hour goal.
- Views, engaged views, average duration, average percentage viewed, subscribers gained/lost and traffic sources.
- Impressions CTR only from Studio; segment by source and upload age.
- Top five videos and top five competitor observations, with dates and source evidence.
- Search terms, topic clusters, title/description/chapters quality and playlist paths.
- First 30 seconds, 1-minute retention, major dips/spikes, average view duration and end-screen survival.
- Cards and end-screen configuration and click metrics.
- Policy, rights and disclosure risks.
- Three or fewer prioritized experiments with baseline, hypothesis, success metric, confidence and review date.
- Conservative/base/upside forecast scenarios using observed performance; label all assumptions and never promise results.

Do not modify published metadata, upload videos or change competitor watchlists without explicit approval.
