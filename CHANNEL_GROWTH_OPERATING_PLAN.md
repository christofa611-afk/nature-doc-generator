## 2. Editorial positioning and defensible audience

Do not compete by making a cheaper imitation of established animated battle-map channels or a high-volume archive narration channel. Build a distinct proposition:

**Cinematic, evidence-led global history told through the decisions, constraints and consequences that shaped conflicts — including theatres and perspectives that receive less sustained English-language documentary coverage.**

Editorial differentiators to test:
- Global coverage: Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Middle East, Eastern Europe and the Pacific, not only Western Europe and the United States.
- Decision-centred storytelling: explain what leaders knew at the time, what options they had, what they misunderstood and what consequences followed.
- Strategic clarity: original, source-checked maps and diagrams that explain geography, logistics, timing and operational choices.
- Human consequences without invented dialogue or melodrama.
- Evidence transparency: source notes, distinctions between established fact, disputed interpretation and uncertainty.
- Cinematic visual continuity: genuine moving footage, original animation and carefully reviewed historical reconstructions; no photo slideshow, still-image zoom or repetitive stock filler.
- Balanced accounts that explain multiple sides without false equivalence or propaganda.

## 5. Cinematic retention design

### Long-form story architecture (target 18–30 minutes; length follows story)
1. **0:00–0:20 — Cold open:** a documented dilemma, reversal, contradiction or consequence. No generic channel intro.
2. **0:20–0:45 — Promise:** state the central question and what the viewer will understand by the end.
3. **0:45–2:00 — Context under pressure:** establish place, stakes, actors and constraints without a long biography dump.
4. **Every 60–90 seconds — Narrative progression:** a new verified fact, decision, map change, consequence, contradiction or shift in the balance.
5. **Middle — Complication:** reveal what the initial explanation misses; test the central hypothesis against evidence.
6. **Final quarter — Consequences:** explain what changed, what did not, and who bore the cost.
7. **Final 20 seconds — Next-story bridge:** deliver a meaningful conclusion, then point to the most relevant next documentary and subscribe element.

Retention is not manufactured by artificial cliffhangers or constant loudness. Use clear causal transitions, varied visual scale, purposeful sound design, and new information at a pace the audience can absorb. Remove repeated claims, redundant recaps and empty cinematic filler.

### Footage rules
- No slideshow or still-image pan/zoom as a substitute for footage.
- Real archival footage only after item-level rights and commercial reuse terms are verified.
- AI-generated photorealistic historical scenes must be genuinely moving, historically plausible and individually reviewed for visual defects.
- Never pass a generated reconstruction off as archival evidence. Label or narrate reconstructions clearly where viewers could mistake them for real footage.
- Use original diagrams and maps with checked borders, dates, locations and source/rights records.
- Keep every scene unique and connected to the narration.

## 6. SEO and packaging system

SEO is not tag stuffing. YouTube says search relevance includes title, description and video content, while engagement and quality also matter; tags are mainly useful for spelling variants. Build the package around one precise viewer question.

For every long-form upload:
- Research one primary search phrase and 3–5 supporting phrases; validate demand and competition before script lock.
- Draft 3 title hypotheses: searchable, curiosity-led and decision/consequence-led. All must accurately describe the video.
- Test 2–3 thumbnail concepts before upload; use one focal subject, readable contrast and minimal text. No fabricated scenes, false quotations or misleading battle outcomes.
- Put the primary phrase naturally in the title and first two description lines.
- Write a unique description with a concise synopsis, chapters, primary sources, rights/attributions, related playlist and a clear next-video link.
- Add only relevant tags and spelling variants; stay within YouTube's tag field limit.
- Use accurate chapters when timestamps are verified; first chapter at 00:00 and at least three ascending timestamps.
- Build a series playlist for connected subjects and link the relevant playlist in the description and end screen.
- Keep titles concise, factual and front-load the key subject.
- Reassess metadata using impressions, CTR, search terms, traffic sources, retention and viewer satisfaction—not raw tags or third-party SEO scores alone.

## 7. End screens and cards — mandatory editor checklist

For every eligible long-form video:
- Reserve a clean visual end-screen area in the final 20 seconds.
- Add one relevant next video or a focused playlist, plus Subscribe.
- Prefer the next episode in the same series or the strongest thematic continuation.
- Check mobile/TV layout, avoid covering captions or important visual action, preview, save and verify in YouTube Studio.
- Record the actual destination and confirmation date in the upload checklist.

Cards:
- Add only when a viewer would genuinely benefit from a related video or playlist at that point in the narrative.
- Use a card near a named topic, related campaign, earlier episode or source explanation; avoid interrupting a critical scene.
- Maximum five cards per video; do not add five merely because the limit allows it.
- Cards are unavailable on Made for Kids videos.
- Verify actual card configuration in YouTube Studio. Do not claim the cards or end screens are live until checked.

A sustainable starting pattern in Africa/Johannesburg time:

- Wednesday: Short derived from a separate, self-contained historical insight; link the relevant long video where the platform allows.
- Friday: Long-form documentary B.

Maintain a two-video buffer of fully reviewed documentaries before promising a cadence. If rights, factual accuracy or quality gates fail, delay rather than publish unsafe or low-quality work.

## 9. Monthly scorecard and interventions

Measure:
- Long-form views per upload at 7, 28 and 90 days.
- Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- Eligible public long-form watch hours.
- Impressions and CTR by Browse, Suggested and Search where available.
- First 30-second retention and key retention dips.
- End-screen element click rate and playlist continuation where available.
- Shorts engaged views and subscribers gained, separately from eligible long-form watch hours.
- Returning viewers, new viewers, subscribers gained/lost, traffic-source mix and top search terms.
- Uploads published versus planned; rights/policy rejection rate.

Use the following diagnostic logic:
- Low impressions + strong retention: improve topic demand, title clarity and distribution pathways; test adjacent audience topics.
- High impressions + weak CTR: test a clearer, more distinctive thumbnail/title pair without exaggerating.
- Good CTR + sharp early drop: align the opening with the promise; remove slow intros and overlong setup.
- Good first minute + mid-video decline: tighten exposition, improve causal transitions and add evidence-led visual changes.
- Good retention + low suggested traffic: build connected series, playlists and end-screen pathways; study audience overlap.
- Strong Short + weak long-form conversion: make the Short's payoff complete, then use a natural related-video bridge; don't assume virality transfers automatically.

Do not impose universal CTR or retention thresholds. Compare each upload with the channel's own history, topic, length, traffic source and audience segment.

## 10. Rights, policy and advertising disclosures

- Zero-policy-violation is the operating objective; absolute zero risk cannot be guaranteed. Pause publication whenever rights, factuality or policy status is uncertain.
- Keep a source register, clip-level rights log, licence snapshots, generation date, model/provider version, prompt/reference records, and reviewer decisions.
- YouTube's inauthentic-content policy rejects mass-produced or repetitive template content; reused content needs meaningful original commentary, substantive transformation or educational value. A licence alone does not guarantee monetization eligibility.
- Disclose realistic synthetic/altered scenes when YouTube requires it. AI-generated photorealistic scenes depicting events or places that did not occur are a likely disclosure case. Do not hide required labels; YouTube states the disclosure itself does not reduce reach or monetization eligibility.
- **Paid promotion is a separate setting.** If there is no payment, sponsorship, product placement, endorsement or other benefit in exchange for promoting a brand, do not select the paid-promotion declaration. If a paid or otherwise compensated brand relationship exists, the declaration and any legally required disclosure are mandatory. Never misclassify a sponsored video to avoid a label.
- Ordinary ads served by YouTube are not, by themselves, creator-paid promotion.
- No unlicensed music, sound effects, footage, maps, fonts, logos or archival images.
- Avoid graphic violence, glorification, extremist praise, dehumanisation, fabricated evidence, misleading metadata and false claims.
- Every video requires factual review, rights review, visual review, disclosure review and final human playback before publication.
